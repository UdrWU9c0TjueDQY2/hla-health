Independent companion activity · DoseBeacon: Pill Reminder

Sort the report from the conclusion

Independent record-reading exercise, using fictional entries only. Draw three columns: reported information, unknown information and next organizational question. Sort these statements: “A helper logged taken at 8:10,” “the phone last updated yesterday,” and “no report appears.” The first describes a report, not observed ingestion. The second describes record freshness. The third leaves what happened unknown. A useful question is which person or record can clarify the history; do not infer or change medication use from this exercise. DoseBeacon provides recurring reminders and distinct report states for the ongoing organization workflow.

Independent companion activity · My Midlife Story: Menopause

Make a three-line visit card

Independent organization exercise; use this fictional example or leave the blanks empty. Write three headings: observation, context and question. Example: “I noted a change on Tuesday”; “I wrote it down after work”; “What information would be useful to bring next time?” Keep guesses out of the observation line, and do not add a diagnosis. Read the card aloud once and remove wording that feels unclear or too personal to share. The result is a conversation aid, not a medical assessment. Keep the original note beside the rewritten card so changes in wording can be checked later.

Independent companion activity · Sturdy: Strength on GLP-1

Map time without prescribing a workout

Independent scheduling activity, not an exercise plan. Draw seven columns for the days of a fictional week. Mark an existing obligation in each column, then circle two open periods that a fictional adult might discuss when planning their own routine. Label the circles “available time,” not “required exercise.” Add a note for equipment access and one unanswered question for the appropriate professional. The output is an availability map only; it does not specify movements, intensity or frequency. Sturdy can organize a user’s ongoing plan once those personal decisions have been made.

Independent companion activity · CraveBreaker: Quit Vaping

Write a neutral refusal in three lengths

Independent adult writing activity. Imagine a fictional gathering where someone offers a vape. Draft three replies: one word, one sentence and one sentence with a topic change. Examples: “No”; “No thanks, that is not for me”; “No thanks—how did your trip go?” Choose wording that sounds natural to the speaker without judging the other person or explaining private health information. Read it once and shorten it if needed. This is communication practice, not a guarantee about cravings or social pressure. CraveBreaker offers ongoing personal planning for adults who choose a quit attempt.

Independent companion activity · Dropkin: Water Reminder

Calculate the amount actually recorded

Independent arithmetic card with a fictional 600 mL bottle. A person records half of it, then a quarter later. Half is 600 × 0.5 = 300 mL; a quarter is 600 × 0.25 = 150 mL. The two entries total 450 mL, or three quarters of that bottle. Label every number and check that the answer does not exceed the stated bottle amount. This exercise is about units and fractions, not a daily drinking target. Dropkin provides saved-vessel logging for recurring records; the paper example does not transfer into the app.

Independent companion activity · Body Unpacked: Health Atlas

Read the basis before comparing the values

Independent data-reading exercise using invented values, not real foods. Sample A lists 6 units per 100 g. Sample B lists 3 units per 50 g. Convert B to the same 100 g basis: 3 × 2 = 6 units. The values now match; the original display did not establish that A contained twice as much on an equal-weight basis. Keep the word “invented” on the worksheet and do not infer nutritional quality. Body Unpacked offers sourced food comparisons; this standalone arithmetic activity teaches why the amount basis must be visible first.

Independent companion activity · Sleepward: Bedtime Routine

Build a fictional evening backwards

Independent scheduling example, not a recommendation about sleep duration or bedtime. A fictional adult chooses to finish an evening plan at 22:30 and reserves 15 minutes for their own closing routine. Subtract 15 minutes to find a routine start of 22:15. If that person also chooses 30 minutes of personal time before it, that block begins at 21:45. Label every time as a fictional preference. The result is a transparent timetable, not a promise of falling asleep. Sleepward can organize a user’s evening choices; this paper example does not transfer into the app.
