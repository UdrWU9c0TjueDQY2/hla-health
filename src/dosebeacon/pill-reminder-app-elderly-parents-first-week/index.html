First-week guide · for the sibling who lives far away · updated November 2026

The 20-minute setup, from three time zones away.

You are not the one in the kitchen with the pill organiser. You are the one who texts “did Mum take her pills?” at 9 pm and gets no answer. A pill reminder app for elderly parents has to work for two people at once: the parent, who needs one big button, and you, who needs a record you can trust without nagging. This is the first week, honestly, with our app.

Who should skip it

  • A parent who will not carry a phone or iPad. Senior Mode needs a device within reach at dose time. A locked dispenser or a daily call may serve better.
  • Families on Android. Each helper needs DoseBeacon on an iPhone or iPad, per the listing.
  • Anyone expecting proof. A tap is a report. If you need verified dispensing, that is hardware and a pharmacy service, not an app.
  • Dose questions. If the question is how much or when, that is the pharmacist or prescriber, not this page.

    The screens, annotated

    What your parent sees

    • 1One card at a time, large and high-contrast. The medicine name, the dose and the instruction, nothing else.
    • 2Four answers: Log as taken, Later, Not sure, I need help. Not sure is a real answer, and the quests reward it.
    • 3Read aloud, Dynamic Type and VoiceOver. Senior Mode never shows ads or purchase screens, per the listing.

    What you see, far away

    • 1Needs action: a dose nobody has reported shows as Not confirmed yet. It does not say missed, because nobody knows yet.
    • 2Conflicting reports are flagged for review rather than one silently winning.
    • 3I'm handling this tells the others you are on it. It is never shown as a dose given.

    The record

    • 1Every entry keeps who reported it, on which device and when. Corrections add to the history; nothing is silently erased.
    • 2On-hand count and refill estimate, clearly labelled as an estimate.
    • 3Archive keeps the history. The 7, 30 or 90-day PDF is what you bring to the doctor.

    The first week, in three moves

    1

    Day 1: set up with them on the phone

    Add the medicines (scan the label, check every field), switch on Senior Mode on their device, run one reminder while you are on the call so they have pressed Log as taken once with you watching.

    2

    Day 2: invite the circle

    A circle code only sends a join request; you approve it and choose a role: manager, dose-logging helper or read-only family. Pick read-only for the relative who worries out loud.

    3

    Days 3 to 7: do nothing dramatic

    Let Not confirmed yet sit for an hour before you call. Review the emergency card together on day 5. On day 7, preview the report, which is the quest that lights the last part of the lighthouse.

    Questions from the far-away sibling

    What is the best pill reminder app for elderly parents?

    The one your parent will actually answer. For the person taking the medicine, that means one big card, large text, read-aloud, and an honest set of answers including Not sure and I need help. For the family, it means a shared record that says who reported what, and does not call a silence a missed dose. DoseBeacon's listing describes exactly that split: Senior Mode for the parent, a Care Circle for the helpers.

    Does it work if my parent's phone has no internet?

    Reminders are scheduled on the device and work offline, per the listing. Sharing to the Care Circle needs a connection; every screen shows when it last updated.

    What does a logged dose actually mean?

    A report. The listing says so in its own words: a logged dose is a report, not proof a medicine was taken. Every report keeps who made it, on which device and when; a dose nobody has reported shows as Not confirmed yet, never missed.

    Can two of us log the same dose by mistake?

    If two people report the same dose it is flagged Needs review rather than overwritten, and tapping I'm handling this is never shown as a dose given. That is the Dose Handshake, which is part of the Family subscription.

    What is free, and what costs money?

    Free: reminders, 1 patient, 2 active medications, 1 helper, full history, reports and the emergency card. DoseBeacon Family adds unlimited active medications, more caregivers and the Dose Handshake at $8.99 a month or $59.99 a year, with a 7-day trial for eligible new subscribers and a reminder before it ends. If you stop subscribing, records stay.

    Is it a medical device?

    No. The listing is explicit: a reminder and record-keeping app, not a medical device; it does not give medical advice or recommend doses. If you are unsure about a dose, ask a pharmacist or the prescriber.
