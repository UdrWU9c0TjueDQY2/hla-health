One week, three siblings

A composite scenario from families we have talked with, not a customer story. Mum is 81, nine pills a day across four times; Ruth lives ten minutes away, Dan forty, Priya in another country.

Monday: the group chat

Dan did mum get her 6pm?Ruth I think so, I was there at 5Priya she told me on the phone she took "the evening ones"Dan ok

Nobody is lying. Nobody knows. "I think so" and "she told me" are the whole record, and the record lives in a thread that also has the plumber's number and a photo of the dog. The CDC's medication safety basics put a current, shared list at the centre of avoiding errors; the group chat is a list nobody can find.

Wednesday: the paper chart on the fridge

Ruth makes a chart. It is good. It has every medicine, the time, and a box to tick. By Wednesday evening it has Mum's tick for the 6 pm, Ruth's tick for the 6 pm, and a note from the carer saying "given 6pm" in the margin. Three records of one dose, or of three doses. The warfarin box has no tick at all, which means nothing or everything.

Dan, forty minutes away, cannot see the fridge. Priya cannot see the country.

Friday: one record, three phones

Ruth sets up DoseBeacon, scans the pharmacy labels into drafts and checks every field before any reminder turns on. She invites Dan and Priya with a circle code; the code only sends a join request, and she approves each one with a role: Dan a dose-logging helper, Priya read-only. Mum's phone goes into Senior Mode: one big card at a time, Log as taken, Later, Not sure, I need help.

6:02 pm Mum taps Log as taken for the evening card.6:04 pm Dan, on the way over, taps "I'm handling this" for the 8 pm. The others see who is on it; the claim is not shown as a dose given.8:10 pm Dan logs the 8 pm. Priya, awake at a strange hour, sees it from her sofa with the time it last updated.

Saturday, Mum taps Not sure on the morning card. It goes into the record as exactly that, not a miss, not a dose, and the quest system quietly rewards the honesty. Ruth rings to check. The chart on the fridge stays up, for the carer, with a note identifying the shared record. Entries still reflect what someone reported.

Agree on one family record

Agree with the person whose record it is about the usual helper, a backup, and which updates each may share. Keep access codes, medication lists, and private health information off the sheet.

Download the family record handoff (PDF, 1 page)

Use the prompts without printing
  1. Identify the current record and how a helper can recognize its latest version.
  2. List the usual record keeper and backup by role, with their agreed responsibilities.
  3. Note which information the person wants shared, and with which role.
  4. For an unclear entry, identify who can clarify it, through which agreed channel, and when.

Example. The usual helper keeps the shared log. The weekend helper reads the latest dated entry. A missing entry stays unknown while the helpers check with the person who made the report.

Honest limits

A logged dose is a report, not proof, and DoseBeacon says so in its own listing: a dose nobody reported shows as "Not confirmed yet", never "missed", and two reports of the same dose are flagged for review rather than merged. The app cannot see a pill go into a mouth, cannot stop a sibling forgetting, and gives no dosing advice. Reminders run on the device without internet, but sharing between phones needs them online. If the question is what Mum should be taking, that is the pharmacist, and the American Geriatrics Society's HealthInAging tip sheet on avoiding overmedication is a good thing to bring to the appointment. This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician.

Who it is not for

  • A parent who will not use a phone at all and has no helper nearby to log for them. The record needs one reporter.
  • Families on Android. Each helper needs DoseBeacon on an iPhone or iPad.
  • Anyone wanting automatic pill-count proof or a dispensing device. It records reports, by design.
  • Anyone looking for an app to decide doses or interactions. It never recommends, and nor do we.

Questions siblings ask

Is there an app for siblings to share a parent's medication schedule?

Yes. DoseBeacon's Care Circle lets you invite siblings, a spouse or a home caregiver with a code that sends a join request; you approve it and choose a role (manager, dose-logging helper or read-only family). Reported doses are shared when phones are online, and every screen shows when it last updated. Each helper needs the app on an iPhone or iPad.

What happens if two of us log the same dose?

It is flagged "Needs review" rather than overwritten, and the record keeps who reported what, on which device, when. A tap of "I'm handling this" tells the others who is on it, and a claim is never shown as a dose given. That is the part the group chat cannot do.

Does a logged dose mean Mum actually took it?

No, and the app says so: a logged dose is a report. A dose nobody reported shows as "Not confirmed yet", never "missed". Corrections add to the history; nothing is silently erased.

Can Mum use it herself?

Senior Mode shows one large high-contrast card at a time with four choices: Log as taken, Later, Not sure, I need help. Read-aloud, Dynamic Type and VoiceOver are supported, reminders are scheduled on the device so they work without internet, and Senior Mode never shows ads or purchase screens.

What is free?

Reminders, one patient, two active medications, one helper, full history, reports and the emergency card, per the listing. More active medications and more caregivers are part of a Family subscription. We describe only what the listing shows.

Does it give medical advice?

No. It is a reminder and record-keeping app, not a medical device, and it never recommends doses or nudges anyone to take more. Neither does this page. This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician or pharmacist.
