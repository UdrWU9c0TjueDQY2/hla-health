Keep the conversation work-shaped.

A useful outline does not need to prove a cause or list every symptom. It can stay anchored to the job: what is difficult, what might help, what you prefer to keep private and when to review.

Impact

Name the task, time or environment—not your entire health story.

Request

Ask to explore one practical change instead of arriving with a long wish list.

Boundary

Decide in advance whether to name menopause or discuss only the work impact.

Review

A check-in gives both sides a concrete point to see what is and is not working.

You can say less.

The planner deliberately offers a work-impact-only opening. It does not tell you what must be disclosed. Your workplace policy, role and location may affect the process, so use official workplace guidance or qualified advice for decisions about rights and formal requests.

Do I have to tell my manager I am in menopause?

This planner does not decide what you should disclose. It helps you draft either a work-impact-only opening or a version that names menopause, depending on your own privacy choice.

Is this legal or HR advice?

No. Workplace policies, duties and processes vary. The tool creates a conversation outline from your choices; it does not determine rights, eligibility or an appropriate workplace adjustment.

Does the planner save my answers?

No. The page builds the draft in the current browser tab and does not send or store the entered text. Closing or refreshing the page clears it.
