Keep the note close to the moment.

A diary is a memory aid. It gives you somewhere to put an observation without having to explain it immediately. You can write an approximate time if that is all you remember, and label it approximate.

Alberta's perimenopause and menopause appointment guidance suggests recording symptoms, when they began, how often they occur, and what you notice around them. It also recommends listing questions and marking your priorities. The sheet below turns that preparation idea into a small, open-ended writing space.

It does not calculate a symptom score, identify a menopause stage or recommend a treatment. If you need care, contact a healthcare professional; you do not need to finish a sheet first.

An original appointment-preparation guide

A note is an observation.
A question leaves room to find out.

Before a routine appointment, turn a few notes into a short agenda: what you noticed, what changed in daily life and what you want to understand. You do not need to complete a diary first. This is a writing aid, not a symptom assessment or a way to decide what caused a change.

One completely fictional example

Main concern: I want help understanding some recent changes and what to follow up.

Observations: On 28 October and 2 November, I woke during the night feeling hot. I did not time either episode. I made no entries for the other nights.

Impact: On 3 November I paused an ordinary work task to reread the instructions. I do not know whether this is connected to those nights.

Three questions for the conversation:

  1. What information would help you assess these changes and the possible explanations?
  2. Which options, if any, would fit my situation and priorities, and what should I understand about their benefits and risks?
  3. What should happen next, when should we review it and who should I contact if things change?

Invented words and dates for a writing example. This is not a patient story, medical recommendation or example of an app-generated diagnosis.

What each part does

Keep a date with the observation.
Two recorded nights are two recorded nights. They are not evidence that every other night was symptom-free. The note says what was not recorded.
Leave uncertainty visible.
“I did not time either episode” is more useful than an invented duration. Add details you genuinely remember; mark estimates as estimates.
Describe impact without declaring a cause.
The work example gives a concrete effect the writer wants to discuss. It does not assert that menopause, poor sleep or anything else caused it.
Ask; do not turn a guess into a result.
The questions invite an assessment and a shared next step. They do not ask the diary or this page to choose a treatment.

Use your own words. If you have many concerns, mark the one you most want to discuss first. Bring any separate information your clinician has asked for.

The source, and the limit

The NHS appointment guide recommends preparing a few important questions, taking notes and clarifying the next step. MyHealth Alberta's appointment preparation also suggests recording symptoms and their timing, then marking priorities. Those are the preparation principles used here; the wording, example and blank agenda are original.

Sources checked 5 November 2026. Neither source endorses this guide or the app. This guide does not diagnose, recommend treatment or tell you to wait for a scheduled visit. Contact a qualified healthcare professional when you need care, even if your notes are incomplete.

Three simple ways to use it.

  1. Write an observation. Keep the date with the words. If you are writing later, say what you remember and where you are unsure.
  2. Keep questions separate. You can wonder about an explanation without recording it as an established fact.
  3. Review before sharing. Circle the issue you most want to discuss. Decide what you want to bring and who should see it.

There is no correct number of entries to complete. The four- and seven-row options are layout choices, not tracking schedules. If your clinician asks you to record particular information, follow that guidance rather than this generic sheet.

Questions before printing.

Does this diary tell me whether I am in menopause?

No. It is an open-ended note sheet, not a diagnostic questionnaire or a validated symptom scale. Discuss symptoms and concerns with a qualified healthcare professional.

Should I fill in missed days from memory?

Only record what you can honestly recall, and identify approximate or uncertain details. Leaving a gap is better than inventing a precise entry. A blank is not the same as a symptom-free day.

Do I need to record treatment details here?

This sheet does not ask for them. Your clinician may want a separate, accurate list of medicines and supplements. Do not use the diary to decide dose changes or treatments.

Will this sheet be saved online?

The page does not ask you to enter notes. Your browser handles printing or saving a blank PDF. Any later handwritten or PDF annotations are outside this tool.
