THE INTERACTIVE WORKSPACE

Compare the dates you actually recorded.

Use 2 to 13 dates in YYYY-MM-DD format, separated by new lines, spaces or commas. The tool sorts dates and rejects duplicates and future dates. Use only dates you know; do not guess missing starts.

Bar widths compare only the intervals in this log. Color carries no medical meaning. The median is the middle interval after sorting; for an even number of intervals, it is the average of the middle two.

Before using this summary: check whether any starts were missed, estimated or entered incorrectly. A missing record can make a gap look longer. These calculations do not establish complete menstrual cycles or explain bleeding.

USE IT IN REAL LIFE

A number is more useful with a note beside it.

For the fictional dates June 1 and June 29, the gap is 28 calendar days. Counting both endpoints as separate days would give the wrong interval. This tool subtracts date-only calendar values, so a daylight-saving clock change does not add or remove a day.

  1. Check the source. Distinguish a date written down at the time from one remembered later.
  2. Keep gaps in the record visible. An absent note is unknown, not proof that nothing happened.
  3. Keep your own context. If you want to discuss a change at an appointment, bring the original dates, relevant notes and your question. The summary does not replace them.

This page cannot determine perimenopause, menopause, pregnancy, causes of bleeding or whether a pattern needs treatment. Discuss medical concerns with a qualified healthcare professional; do not use this arithmetic to make medical or contraception decisions.
