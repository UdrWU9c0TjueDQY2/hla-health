How it works, three steps

1

One tap when something happens

Hot flash, foggy, poor sleep, overwhelmed: one tap saves the moment with its date and time. Add words if you want. Missing days stay unknown, not symptom-free.

2

Keep the questions

“Can we talk about the night waking?” saved the moment you think of it, not forgotten in the waiting room. “Something changed” marks a shift in a symptom or a treatment your clinician prescribed; later notes never overwrite earlier ones.

3

Walk in with one page

Pick the main concern, up to three questions and the notes you want to share. Preview, edit, save as PDF, print, or share only when you decide.

Questions people ask next

Which menopause tracking app does not need an account?

Of the five compared here, My Midlife Story's listing says no account, no ads, no tracking and no automatic health-data uploads, and Menelle's says privacy-focused with secure local storage. The other three listings describe communities or personalised plans and do not state how data is stored; read their privacy policies before you decide, and treat “listing does not say” as exactly that.

What should I log before a doctor's appointment?

What changed, when, and what you want to ask. You do not need a daily diary: My Midlife Story's own FAQ says save a note only when something is worth remembering, and missing days stay unknown rather than symptom-free. The sample brief above shows the shape: main concern, up to three questions, the notes you choose.

Does the app diagnose perimenopause or recommend HRT?

No. The listing says it organises your own notes and questions and does not diagnose, recommend treatment or doses, or say one thing caused another. That is the right boundary for any tracker; a clinician does the rest.

Is it free?

Logging, history, search, visit briefs, PDF export, reminders, encrypted backup and deletion are free, per the listing, and records are never hidden if Premium ends. Premium is a monthly or annual subscription for comparisons between periods, grouped Replays, advanced briefs, Playbooks, a yearly Passport and garden looks.

Is there anything for the days when even one tap is too much?

Low-Capacity mode: larger, calmer controls, one main action at a time, motion and sounds turned down. It works with VoiceOver, Dynamic Type and Reduce Motion, per the listing.

Why a garden?

Because streaks are the wrong motivation for symptoms. The listing says points never depend on symptoms or how you feel, a break never removes progress, and blooms never wilt. The garden grows from notes saved, not from having a bad night.
