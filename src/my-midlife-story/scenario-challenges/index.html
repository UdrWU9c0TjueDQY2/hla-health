Personal Notes, Clear Conversations

27 scenarios about accurate timelines, private summaries and focused appointment questions.

Choose a response. See the reasoning.

Read the facts, pick the answer that best fits the stated goal, then open the explanation. Start with five or browse the full set. There is no timer or personal assessment; the useful part is understanding the choice.

These are original web companion exercises. They are independent of the app; answers and progress do not transfer. All people and records in the scenarios are fictional. They practise organization and interpreting supplied information, not diagnosis, treatment, dose decisions or health assessment.

A report was issued on June 2 and copied into a journal on June 9. The writer wants both dates preserved accurately, because the source's date and the day of transcription describe different events in the history of the personal record.

Show the worked answer

B. Source June 2; copied June 9

The two dates belong to different events. Labeling each preserves the report's origin and the journal's later transcription, while assigning one date to both would lose part of the record's documented history unnecessarily.

The final summary needs its limits

A person prepares a summary from notes containing confirmed observations, approximate dates and missing days. The goal is a useful professional conversation that preserves those differences, rather than making the shortened summary appear more complete or precise than its original source material.

Show the worked answer

C. Keep estimates and gaps labeled in the summary

A summary should retain limitations that affect interpretation of the original record. Keeping estimates and gaps visible preserves those distinctions, whereas removing labels or filling gaps would create unsupported certainty in the shortened account.
