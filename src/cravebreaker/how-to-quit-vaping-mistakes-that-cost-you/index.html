  18. The six mistakes, in one list

  19. You, with a plan
    1. Counting from zero after one slip

      One puff on day 12 becomes "I failed" becomes a week of vaping. The slip cost one puff; the story cost the week.

      Fix: Keep the quit date and the total. Record the lapse, look at what happened, choose the next step. A slip is a temporary setback, not a reset (Smokefree.gov).

    2. Waiting for willpower when the craving hits

      At 11:40 pm nobody has willpower. You have ninety seconds and a phone.

      Fix: Have the plan ready before the craving: ten slow breaths, a short walk, switch activity, text someone. Smokefree.gov's list is the same list CraveBreaker puts behind one tap.

    3. Never pricing it

      If you do not know what a week of vaping costs you, the money leaves without a sound, and so does the main reason many adults quit.

      Fix: Enter what you spend per week. See the daily and yearly figure. Then set the money aside for real, not on paper. The estimator above shows the formula.

    4. Quitting without knowing your triggers

      Coffee, the car, the work break, the argument. If you cannot name them, you walk into them.

      Fix: Log each craving: situation, intensity, what you tried. Patterns appear once there is enough data, as observations, not causes. The app does this in a few seconds per craving.

    5. Telling no one

      A private quit has no one to text at 11:40 pm.

      Fix: Pick one person and tell them the date. Smokefree.gov lists contacting supportive people as a coping action; the app's Reach Out tool opens a text.

    6. Going it alone when free help exists

      Quitlines and chat support are free and staffed by trained people; most people quitting never call.

      Fix: 1-800-QUIT-NOW, NCI LiveHelp, and a doctor or pharmacist about medication. The app keeps the numbers one tap away; it does not replace them.

  20. Questions from the same night

  21. You, with a plan
    How long does a vape craving last?

    Smokefree.gov describes cravings as common but temporary, and says they fade the longer you stay quit. The practical version: do one thing for a few minutes (ten slow breaths, a walk, a text to someone) and the urge usually passes before the thing is done. CraveBreaker's craving timer runs 1, 3 or 5 minutes or your own length for exactly that reason.

    I vaped once after quitting. Do I start over?

    No. Smokefree.gov calls a slip a temporary setback, not a failure, and says you are not back at the start. CraveBreaker keeps your original quit date and total vape-free days when you record a lapse; only the current stretch resets. Look at what happened, adjust the plan, pick the next step.

    Is there medication for quitting vaping?

    Nicotine replacement, varenicline and bupropion are used for quitting cigarettes and can reduce cravings and withdrawal; Smokefree.gov notes the FDA has not approved them for quitting vaping and suggests talking with a doctor or pharmacist. This page and the app are not medical advice.

    What does CraveBreaker cost?

    Every craving tool is free, per the listing, along with the quit plan, log, journal, export, progress, basic patterns, the savings estimate and the first 7 missions. Premium is optional: all 30 missions and the 66-day path, preparation sessions, detailed insights and a money report, as a weekly or annual subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase.

    Is my data private?

    The listing says no account, no ads, no analytics; logs, journal, reasons and plan stay on the iPhone and work offline, and you can export or delete everything from Settings. Purchases go through Apple.

    Where can I get free help right now?

    In the U.S., 1-800-QUIT-NOW (1-800-784-8669) connects you to your state quitline, and the National Cancer Institute runs a free LiveHelp chat. Both are listed on Smokefree.gov. The app links to U.S. and Canada quitline information for free.
