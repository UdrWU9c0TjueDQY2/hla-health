How much water should you drink in winter? Less than you think is the common answer and it is the wrong one: cold blunts thirst, not need. Six myths checked against named sources, then a live pace counter for your own goal and hours, for people who work, run or ski outside when it is cold.

Six mythsSources at the end
Myth

You need less water in winter.

Fact

Your thirst drops; your need does not drop as much. University of New Hampshire research found cold reduces thirst sensation by up to 40 percent, and fluid deficits of 3 to 8 percent of body mass have been reported in people working in the cold.

Kenefick, UNH, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise
Myth

Coffee and tea dehydrate you.

Fact

They count. Mayo Clinic: other liquids such as tea, coffee and milk count toward how much you should drink each day.

Mayo Clinic
Myth

Everyone needs eight glasses.

Fact

Mayo Clinic calls eight glasses a good goal, then says some people may need less and others more. Total fluid for the average healthy adult is about 11.5 to 15.5 cups (2.7 to 3.7 litres) from all sources, and food provides about 20 percent of it.

Mayo Clinic
Myth

If you are not thirsty, you are fine.

Fact

In the cold, thirst is a poor alarm. The UNH study describes the mechanism: blood moves to the core, the brain misses the signal, and you do not feel like drinking even while losing fluid. A routine or a pace replaces the alarm.

Kenefick, UNH
Myth

More is always better.

Fact

Drinking too much is rare and typically not a problem for healthy adults, Mayo Clinic says, but it can cause low sodium (hyponatremia), which can be life-threatening. Dropkin, by design, earns you nothing for water past your goal.

Mayo Clinic; App Store listing
Myth

An app can tell you how much you need.

Fact

No. Dropkin’s own listing says it is not a medical device, does not provide medical advice and does not determine your hydration needs. You pick a goal; the app tracks it. If you have a condition that limits fluids, your doctor sets the number.

QuestionsShort answers
How much water should you drink in winter?

About as much as in any other season for the same activity, and more than your thirst suggests. The average healthy adult’s total fluid is roughly 2.7 to 3.7 litres a day from all sources including food (Mayo Clinic); cold cuts thirst by up to 40 percent (UNH), so a routine matters more in winter, not less. Pick a goal that fits your size and your day, and check it with a doctor if you have a condition.

Why am I never thirsty when it is cold?

Cold narrows the blood vessels in your extremities, blood volume at the core rises, and the brain does not detect the drop that normally triggers thirst and the hormone that makes your kidneys hold water. That is the UNH finding in one sentence.

Does the pace counter above store anything?

No. It runs in your browser from the numbers you typed and resets on reload. Dropkin’s Hydration Pace does the same calculation against your wake and sleep hours and shows On pace or A little behind; never red, never a lecture, per the listing.

Either. The listing says you pick oz or mL and litres on the first screen, bottle presets come in round numbers for your unit, and switching later converts your history exactly.

Will it nag me?
