Questions people ask

How many 16 oz bottles of water should I drink a day?

For a desk day, roughly four to five 16 oz bottles covers the NHS 6-to-8-glasses guide and sits inside the Mayo Clinic range once you count food and other drinks. A gym day or a hot day adds one or two. The chart above does the arithmetic for your bottle and your day.

How many 500 ml bottles a day?

About four for an ordinary day, five to six on a day you sweat. Half-litre bottles make the sums easy, which is one reason they are the default in Dropkin.

Does coffee count toward water intake?

Yes. The NHS lists tea and coffee among drinks that count toward your daily fluid. The caffeine myth comes from old studies at very high doses. Dropkin lets you log any drink, not only water.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes. Drinking far beyond thirst over a short time, especially during long endurance events, can dilute blood sodium, which is dangerous. The ranges here are modest; if you are routinely drinking much more and still thirsty, that is a question for a clinician.

Should I drink more on a workout day?

Mayo Clinic advises drinking extra before, during and after exercise to replace what you sweat. How much extra depends on how hard, how long and how hot. The chart adds a rule-of-thumb bottle per hard hour; your weight before and after a session is a better personal measure than any calculator.

Does Dropkin tell me how much to drink?

It lets you set a goal, in round numbers for your unit, and shows whether you are on pace against your own waking hours. It never turns red and never lectures. It does not diagnose or prescribe, and neither does this page: this is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician.

  1. Water Counts fully. The NHS guide puts it first for a reason: nothing added.
  2. Lower-fat milk Counts. The NHS lists it alongside water as a good choice.
  3. Tea or coffee Counts toward your fluids, per the NHS. The old idea that caffeine cancels it out is not what the guidance says.
  4. Fruit juice Counts as fluid, but the NHS advises keeping it to a small glass a day because of the sugar.
  5. Fizzy drink It is fluid, so it counts, and it is the one the guidance tells you to limit most for sugar and teeth.
  6. Beer Alcohol makes you pass more water than usual. It is not a way to hit a hydration goal, whatever the pub says.
