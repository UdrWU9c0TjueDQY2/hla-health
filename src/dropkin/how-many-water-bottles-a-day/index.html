What one tap looks like

How it works, in three steps

  1. Pick your unit and your bottle. Ounces or millilitres on the first screen; bottle sizes and goal steps use round numbers in your unit, and your history converts exactly if you switch.
  2. Tap when you finish it. Half and quarter buttons sit beside the big one; hold for three-quarters or a custom amount. Every log has Undo, and History lets you edit or delete any drink.
  3. Let the pace do the nagging, gently. Hydration Pace shows On pace or A little behind against your own wake-to-sleep hours. Reminders wait after each drink, stop at your goal, and get quieter if you ignore them, never louder.

Honest limits

The calculator only divides the amount you enter by the selected bottle capacity. It does not account for food, other drinks, health conditions, medication, heat or exercise, and it does not assess hydration.

Who it is not for

  • Anyone on a fluid restriction. Your clinician's number wins.
  • Endurance athletes who need sodium and sweat-rate planning. A bottle counter is not a race plan.
  • People who want a social feed, streak shaming or a red screen when they miss. Dropkin is built to never turn red.
  • Android. It is an iPhone app.

How many bottles does an amount equal?

How many 500 ml bottles are in 1500 ml?

Three: 1500 ÷ 500 = 3. This is a worked conversion, not a daily drinking recommendation.

What does a fraction of a bottle mean?

A result of 2.5 means two full bottles and half of another. Results show up to three decimal places; use the written remainder for a practical amount in ml.

Does this calculate how much I should drink?

No. Fluid needs vary. The NHS gives general guidance and notes that circumstances such as heat and activity can change needs; it does not provide the day-based bottle formulas previously used on this page.

Do other drinks count?

The NHS says drinks such as milk, tea and coffee contribute to fluid intake, and food also supplies some fluid. A water-bottle count alone does not describe all fluid intake.
