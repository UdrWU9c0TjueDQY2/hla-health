Keep a set-by-set record

Record one exercise from a session you already did. Give each set a status so completed repetitions, skipped sets and missing entries stay distinct. Save the note to take it with you.

Load is optional. Enter the weight you actually recorded and use the same unit throughout. A blank load stays unspecified. This tool records past activity without suggesting a workout.


Write what happened.
A plan is an intention, not a completed log. If you planned eight repetitions but recorded six, preserve the six. If you stopped a session early, mark that the session ended early rather than quietly turning the remaining plan into completed work.
When a movement changes, name the replacement instead of pretending it was the original movement. If a load was recorded, include its unit and what it refers to. “5 kg per dumbbell” and “5 kg total” are not interchangeable descriptions.
Keep uncertainty visible.
An empty field may mean not started, forgotten or not recorded. Choose the state you actually know. If you cannot reconstruct the missing detail, “not recorded” is more honest than a guessed number.
A total made from available entries is a recorded subtotal when part of the session is unknown. It is not proof of everything that happened. This distinction matters when you look back or prepare a summary for someone else.

Six lines worth keeping
  1. Session date: when this record refers to.
  2. Original plan: the activity or session you intended.
  3. Actually completed: the work you can report, using clear units.
  4. Changed or not started: what differed from the plan.
  5. Unknown: details you did not record or cannot recall.
  6. Context or questions: a short factual note, without inventing a medical explanation.
Fictional example: “Planned three sets. Recorded 8 and 6 repetitions. The session ended before the third set began. No third-set repetitions were completed.” That is a clear account of this example, not a judgement about whether the session was enough.
Does a smaller recorded number mean I lost strength?
This resource draws no conclusion about strength, health or the effect of medication. A log entry is a self-recorded observation. Different movements, loads, conditions and incomplete records should not be treated as identical measurements.
Should I finish the skipped work later?
This worksheet does not prescribe exercise or decide whether continuing is appropriate. Follow your existing professional guidance and the needs of your situation. Do not use a recordkeeping total as a reason to push through symptoms.
